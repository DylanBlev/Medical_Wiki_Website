Bloodwork and Lab Tests

Bloodwork is one of the most common medical procedures used to help doctors understand how the body is functioning. Blood tests can check for infections, vitamin deficiencies, blood sugar levels, organ function, and many other health conditions.

Many people feel nervous about bloodwork because of needles or fear of discomfort. Understanding the process ahead of time can help reduce anxiety and make the experience feel more manageable.

Why Blood Tests Are Performed

Doctors may order bloodwork during routine checkups, before surgeries, or when diagnosing symptoms. Common reasons for blood tests include:

  • Checking overall health
  • Monitoring medications or treatments
  • Testing for infections or illnesses
  • Measuring cholesterol or blood sugar levels
  • Evaluating organ function

What Happens During Bloodwork?

A healthcare worker, often called a phlebotomist, will collect a small sample of blood. The process usually takes only a few minutes.

  • You may sit in a chair with your arm extended
  • A band called a tourniquet is placed around your upper arm
  • The skin is cleaned with an alcohol wipe
  • A small needle is inserted into a vein to collect blood
  • The needle is removed and a bandage is applied
Helpful Tip: Most people feel only a quick pinch or brief pressure when the needle is inserted. The discomfort usually lasts just a few seconds.

Preparing for Bloodwork

  • Drink water before your appointment unless instructed otherwise
  • Follow fasting instructions if required
  • Wear clothing with sleeves that can easily roll up
  • Tell staff if you are afraid of needles or have fainted before

Managing Anxiety During Blood Tests

Feeling anxious during bloodwork is very common. Some people may feel nervous about needles, seeing blood, or medical environments.

To help reduce stress:

  • Take slow, deep breaths
  • Look away during the blood draw if it helps
  • Listen to music or focus on a calming thought
  • Let the healthcare worker know if you feel dizzy or anxious
  • Remain seated for a few moments afterward if needed

Healthcare workers perform blood draws every day and are trained to help patients feel comfortable and safe during the process.

After the Blood Test

You may have slight soreness or a small bruise where the needle was inserted, but this usually fades quickly.

Most people can return to normal activities immediately after bloodwork unless told otherwise. Test results are usually reviewed by your healthcare provider during a follow-up appointment or through a patient portal.

Blood tests are quick, routine procedures that provide important information about your health. Understanding the process ahead of time can help make bloodwork feel less intimidating and easier to handle.
