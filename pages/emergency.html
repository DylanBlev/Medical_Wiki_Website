Emergency Room Visits

back

What is it?

An emergency room (ER) visit can feel stressful and overwhelming, especially during unexpected situations. Emergency departments are designed to provide immediate medical care for serious injuries, illnesses, or symptoms that require urgent attention.

Understanding what happens during an emergency visit can help reduce anxiety and make the experience feel more manageable during a difficult time.

Important: If you are experiencing severe chest pain, difficulty breathing, heavy bleeding, signs of a stroke, loss of consciousness, or another life-threatening emergency, call emergency services immediately.

What Happens When You Arrive?

After arriving at the emergency room, you will usually go through a process called triage. A nurse or medical professional will quickly assess your symptoms and determine how urgently you need treatment.

Patients with life-threatening conditions are treated first, which means wait times may vary depending on the seriousness of each patient's condition.

Common Steps During an ER Visit

  • Checking vital signs such as heart rate, blood pressure, and temperature
  • Discussing symptoms and medical history
  • Performing physical examinations
  • Ordering blood tests, X-rays, or scans if needed
  • Providing medication, fluids, or treatment
  • Monitoring your condition while waiting for results

Common Emergency Procedures

Blood Tests

Blood samples may be taken to check for infections, injuries, or other medical concerns. You may feel a quick pinch when the needle is inserted.

X-Rays and Imaging

Imaging tests such as X-rays, CT scans, or MRIs help doctors examine bones, organs, and internal injuries.

Stitches

Cuts or wounds may need stitches to help the skin heal properly. The area is usually numbed before the procedure to reduce discomfort.

Managing Anxiety in the Emergency Room

It is completely normal to feel nervous or scared during an emergency. Hospitals can feel busy, loud, and unfamiliar. To help reduce stress:

  • Take slow, steady breaths
  • Ask staff to explain procedures clearly
  • Focus on one step at a time
  • Bring a trusted family member or friend if possible
  • Inform staff if you feel overwhelmed or anxious

Healthcare workers are trained to support patients during emergencies and can answer questions to help you feel more informed and comfortable.

After the Visit

Before leaving the emergency room, you may receive:

  • Discharge instructions
  • Prescriptions or medications
  • Recommendations for follow-up care
  • Warning signs to watch for at home

Be sure to carefully follow instructions and contact a healthcare provider if symptoms worsen after returning home.

Emergency rooms are designed to provide fast and effective care during urgent situations. Knowing what to expect ahead of time can help make emergency visits feel less frightening and more understandable.
