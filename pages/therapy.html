Therapy and Counseling

Therapy, also called counseling or psychotherapy, is a supportive process where individuals speak with a trained mental health professional about emotions, thoughts, stress, relationships, or personal challenges. Therapy can help people better understand themselves, develop coping skills, and improve emotional well-being.

Starting therapy can feel intimidating, especially if you have never attended a session before. Understanding what happens during therapy can help reduce anxiety and make the experience feel more comfortable and approachable.

What Happens During a Therapy Session?

During a therapy appointment, the therapist may ask questions about:

  • Your emotions and current concerns
  • Stress at school, work, or home
  • Relationships and social experiences
  • Your goals for therapy
  • Past experiences that may affect your mental health

Sessions are usually conversational and take place in a private, confidential setting. Most therapy appointments last between 45 and 60 minutes.

Common Types of Therapy

Talk Therapy

Talk therapy focuses on discussing feelings, thoughts, and experiences in a safe and supportive environment.

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

CBT helps people identify and change negative thought patterns and behaviors that may contribute to stress, anxiety, or depression.

Group Therapy

Group therapy involves multiple participants who share experiences and support one another under the guidance of a therapist.

Family Therapy

Family therapy focuses on improving communication and resolving conflicts between family members.

How to Prepare for Therapy

  • Think about what you would like to discuss
  • Bring notes or questions if helpful
  • Wear comfortable clothing
  • Remember that it is okay to feel nervous
Helpful Reminder: You do not need to have everything figured out before starting therapy. Therapists are trained to guide conversations at a pace that feels comfortable for you.

Managing Anxiety About Therapy

Many people feel anxious before their first session. Common worries include being judged, not knowing what to say, or feeling emotional during the appointment.

To help reduce anxiety:

  • Remember that therapy is a judgment-free space
  • Be honest about feeling nervous
  • Take your time answering questions
  • Understand that silence and emotions are normal during sessions

Therapists are trained to create a supportive environment and help clients feel safe, respected, and understood.

After the Session

After therapy, some people feel relieved, emotional, thoughtful, or mentally tired. These reactions are normal. Your therapist may suggest coping strategies, journaling, relaxation exercises, or goals to work on between sessions.

Therapy is a process that takes time, and progress often happens gradually. Learning what to expect can help make the experience feel less overwhelming and more empowering.
