What is it?

Medical scans and imaging tests help doctors look inside the body to diagnose injuries, illnesses, and other health conditions. These procedures are very common and are usually safe, non-invasive, and painless.

Many people feel nervous before a scan because they are unsure what the machines look like or how the procedure works. Understanding what to expect can help reduce anxiety and make the experience feel more manageable.

Common Types of Medical Scans

X-Rays

X-rays use small amounts of radiation to create images of bones and certain tissues. They are commonly used to detect fractures, infections, or dental problems.

CT Scans

A CT scan combines multiple X-ray images to create detailed pictures of the body. During the scan, you usually lie still on a table that moves through a large ring-shaped machine.

MRI Scans

MRI scans use magnets and radio waves to create detailed images of organs, muscles, and soft tissues. MRI machines can be loud, but headphones or ear protection are often provided.

Ultrasounds

Ultrasounds use sound waves to create live images inside the body. A technician moves a handheld device over the skin using a special gel.

What Happens During a Scan?

  • You may be asked to change into a hospital gown
  • Staff may remove jewelry or metal objects
  • You will be guided into the scanning area
  • Technicians will explain instructions before starting
  • You may need to stay still for clear images
Helpful Tip: Most scans are painless. Some may feel uncomfortable due to staying still for several minutes, but the procedures themselves usually do not hurt.

Preparing for a Medical Scan

  • Wear comfortable clothing
  • Follow instructions about eating or drinking beforehand
  • Inform staff about allergies, pregnancy, or medical implants
  • Arrive early for paperwork or preparation

Managing Anxiety During Scans

It is common to feel nervous during imaging procedures, especially inside MRI machines or unfamiliar medical environments.

To help reduce stress:

  • Practice slow, steady breathing
  • Ask staff to explain each step before the scan begins
  • Close your eyes and focus on relaxing
  • Use headphones or calming music if available
  • Tell staff if you feel claustrophobic or uncomfortable

Medical imaging staff are trained to help patients feel safe and comfortable throughout the procedure.

After the Scan

Most scans allow patients to return to normal activities immediately afterward. A doctor will review the images and discuss the results during a follow-up appointment or phone call.

Medical scans are important tools that help healthcare providers diagnose and treat conditions accurately. Knowing what to expect can help make imaging procedures feel less intimidating and easier to manage.
